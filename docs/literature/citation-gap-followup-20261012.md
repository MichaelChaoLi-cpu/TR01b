## Candidate papers

### 1. Kawasaki et al. (2022)

**Estimating freight production/attraction using nighttime satellite imagery.**
*Communications in Transportation Research*, 2, 100067.

DOI: [10.1016/j.commtr.2022.100067](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.commtr.2022.100067).
[Publisher abstract](https://www.sciopen.com/article/10.1016/j.commtr.2022.100067).
[Publisher full text](https://file.sciopen.com/sciopen_public/1625394896726216706.pdf).

This Japanese study estimates prefectural freight production and attraction
using nighttime light indicators and other predictors. Its abstract identifies
differences between light aggregations and calls for further examination of
spatial transferability. The conclusions on printed pages 10–11 discuss freight
production in darker areas and discrepancies for Aichi and Tokyo.

Relevant target: `review-activity-proxy-p01-s05`.
This provides transport-specific evidence for distinguishing a light signal
from a calibrated freight estimate. It does not validate road-user counts in
individual Japanese meshes or measure the welfare value of journeys.

### 2. Wardman, Chintakayala, and de Jong (2016)

**Values of travel time in Europe: Review and meta-analysis.**
*Transportation Research Part A: Policy and Practice*, 94, 93–111.

DOI: [10.1016/j.tra.2016.08.019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tra.2016.08.019).
[Author-repository record and accepted manuscript](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/104595/).

The abstract reports that monetary time valuations differ by travel purpose,
mode, distance, type of time, and other characteristics. It offers a relevant
transport-appraisal source for the journey-value component of
`review-activity-proxy-p01-s05`.

Time valuation is one component of transport benefits; this paper does not
estimate total journey value from nighttime radiance. Pairing it with a light
proxy study provides background for a measurement distinction, not a direct
empirical test of the manuscript's complete compound sentence.

### 3. Wells (1983)

**Determining Maintenance Needs of County Roads and City Streets (Abridgment).**
*Transportation Research Record*, 943, 27–30.

[TRB original paper](https://onlinepubs.trb.org/Onlinepubs/trr/1983/943/943-006.pdf).
[TRB bibliographic record](https://trid.trb.org/View/204574).
No DOI was found in the official record; use the verified publisher URL.

The abstract explicitly separates ongoing maintenance needs and accumulated
backlog costs, then compares both with actual expenditures in Bay Area cities
and counties. This is the most direct candidate found for the distinction
between observed spending, asset-related needs, and deferred maintenance.

Relevant targets: `review-maintenance-context-p01-s02` and
`data-municipal-measurement-scope-p01-s08`.
The study's empirical shortfall is a historical US case. It must not be
presented as an estimate of Japanese maintenance liabilities. Its distinction
between spending and need does not by itself establish a universal ordering
of municipal maintenance-spending ratios.

### 4. Harvey (2024)

**Estimating from an optimisation model the cost of restricting road
maintenance funding for a large network.**
*International Journal of Pavement Engineering*, 25(1), 2400540.

DOI: [10.1080/10298436.2024.2400540](https://doi.org/10.1080/10298436.2024.2400540).
[Publisher article and abstract](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10298436.2024.2400540).

This published journal article compares economically optimal maintenance
spending under unconstrained, present-value-constrained, and annually
constrained funding scenarios. It provides evidence that budget restrictions
affect selected maintenance plans and that relaxing a constraint has an
economic value.

Relevant targets: `data-municipal-measurement-scope-p01-s08` and
`discussion-budget-context-p01-s08`, with appropriate qualification.
Its spending scenarios are optimization outputs, not observed municipal
accounts. It supports discussion of constrained allocation versus maintenance
planning, not a claim that every high spending share indicates greater asset
needs or that every low share proves underfunding. This is a 2024 journal
article, separate from the previously discussed 2012 OECD working paper.

## Recent substitute for Wells (1983)

Human decision UNA-D-20261012-007 requests a more recent journal-paper
substitute because Wells (1983) is too old. The earlier shortlist is retained
as search history; Wells is no longer the recommended maintenance candidate.

**Hamalainen, Vaismaa, and Kolisoja (2025). Exploring information needed in
maintenance backlog-related decision-making in the Finnish road network.**
*Built Environment Project and Asset Management*, 15(2), 271–287.

DOI: [10.1108/BEPAM-05-2024-0131](https://doi.org/10.1108/BEPAM-05-2024-0131).
[Author-university record, abstract, and open-access published paper](https://researchportal.tuni.fi/en/publications/exploring-information-needed-in-maintenance-backlog-related-decis/).

The verified abstract reports 21 stakeholder interviews in Finland. It
distinguishes operational information about road condition from higher-level
use of a monetary backlog measure as a budgeting input, and identifies
insufficient maintenance funding as one contributor to backlog growth.
This is a recent peer-reviewed journal article, not a working paper.

It is a candidate for the maintenance-needs and budgeting context, alongside
the already recommended Harvey (2024). It does not directly compare observed
municipal spending against independently measured maintenance requirements
as Wells did. Neither candidate, alone or paired, directly validates an
ordering of municipalities' maintenance needs from expenditure shares.
The newer candidate is outside the user's preferred JTG, Transportation
Research, and Transport Policy journals. Its recommendation and primary-source
verification do not constitute a completed citation-support assignment.
